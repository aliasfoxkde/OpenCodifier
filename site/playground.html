Maze Solver & Dungeon Crawler — what you are actually watching

Every step the bot takes is a decision made the same way the runtime makes them: cheapest reliable mechanism first. There is no neural network in this maze. The scout checks a rule (fight is forced, exit is one step away, a monster is too close), then falls back to breadth-first searches over only what it has seen — exit route, loot, chest, nearest unexplored frontier. When two frontier routes cost exactly the same, and you have the engine checkbox on, that genuine tie becomes a real choice request to the same WASM engine the panel below runs. The chip next to the canvas names the rung that decided every single step. Two map types — classic corridors, or rooms-and-hallways dungeons — and two ways to see: a full disc, or a facing torch cone where turning matters.

How to play: pick play (WASD / arrows, or the d-pad), or versus to race a glowing ghost of the bot that runs its own identical maze with its own fog — it gains no information from you, and you none from it. Loot piles score, chests open in one second (coins 0–5… or something with teeth), monsters chase what they can see along a ray and cost you a two-second static fight on contact, and the optional timer ends the run when it expires — unless you switch on avoid monsters, which routes the walker around every threat it has actually seen (and nothing it hasn't; the fog stays honest). Found everything? The exit is the one opening in the outer wall — usually as far from the middle as it can be.

Why it is here: to show the methodology moving. Real maze games hand the AI omniscience; this one hands the bot the same darkness you get, and you can audit every choice it made in the trace panel. The leaderboard is session-only — nothing you do here is stored, sent, or scored anywhere but your tab.

you the bot (ghost in versus) exit loot chest — 1 s, coins or teeth monster — 2 s fight unseen remembered

Honest footnotes: vision is generalized Bresenham line-of-sight per cell, walls stop the ray. The maze itself, the bot's exploration ladder, and the scoring arithmetic are plain, tested JavaScript (site/maze-core.js, 70 headless tests) — only the tie-breaks go to WASM. God view is a spectator cheat: any run finished while it is on is marked assisted and scores nothing. Thinking about more than a demo — progression, real combat, multiplayer — is sketched in docs/planning/maze-solver/STANDALONE-GAME.md.

One runtime under all of it

The games share a small SDK — seeded RNG, fixed-timestep loop, config normalization, rubric scoring, the engine adapter, and the UI shell — so a new game is mostly its own map, rules, and rendering. The same SDK doubles as the honest way to use the engine outside games: deterministic seeds as test fixtures, verdict gates in CI, and plain typed decisions with calibrated confidence anywhere a choice needs an audit trail. The maze's methodology is documented end to end in docs/planning/playground/DECISIONS-SDK-PLAN.md.
