MicroWorld — the colony thinks out loud

One tiny deterministic world. The creatures are the decision agents: every ~0.75 s each re-scores ten actions from its own meters and its eight-cell view — eat, drink, flee, fight, explore, rest, gather, go home, follow, ignore — and a genuine near-tie goes to the engine over just the tied candidates, under a colony-wide budget. The fox hunts by rule and is driven off by packs; births cost the colony 6 stores; the grass regrows. Click a creature to pin its decision table to the trace line and watch a colony think.

What decides what

  • Creatures decide; everything else is a rule. Each creature re-scores ten actions about every 0.75 s from its meters (energy, water, carrying), the fox's distance, its pack size, the grass under it and the ponds in sight. Fleeing outranks everything when the fox is within seven cells; a pack of two or more may choose to fight; a carrier scores going home.
  • Near-ties go to the engine, on the record. When the top two scored actions finish within 1.5 points, the request carries only the tied candidates with the creature's facts on the state — if the colony-wide budget has a token (2 asks/s by default). A refusal falls back to the argmax without derailing anyone, and the rung chip says which rung answered.
  • The fox is a rule, never a question. It hunts the nearest creature in sight; two or more creatures within two cells drive it off. No request that names the fox as prey reaches the engine — the pack rule answers first.
  • Population is the scoreboard. A newborn costs the colony 6 stores; carriers deposit at the home plot; starvation, thirst and the fox all subtract. The board records a checkpoint every 60 sim-seconds, and extinction is a verdict, not an error.

Honest edges

  • The engine is a budgeted voice, not the boss: with the default budget of 2 asks/s, most decisions land on the argmax rule and every one of them says so. Set the budget to 0 and the colony runs entirely on rules.
  • The world is small on purpose: 48×30 cells, a two-cell sight advantage for the fox, and no long-term memory beyond a fading visit map. It is a decision demonstrator, not an ecology — complexity beyond this is a different project.
  • Determinism is test-pinned: same seed, same world — every decision, deposit, birth and death replays exactly. The engine only ever chooses between tied candidates the rules proposed; it never invents an action the scoring did not offer.
  • The leaderboard is session-only, the leaderboard is the population curve, and nothing you do here is stored, sent, or scored anywhere but your tab.
